04

How I work

  1. Compare models, don’t just fit one

    For the housing project I built several regression models and scored them on adjusted R², RMSE and MAE before choosing one. Adding variables always pushes plain R² up, so a single model with a good-looking fit statistic tells you very little on its own.

  2. Accuracy is not the same as usefulness

    In the credit risk data 78% of loans were repaid, so a model that predicts “no default” every time is 78% accurate and completely useless. I lead with the base rate so a headline metric cannot hide what it is not telling you.

  3. Let the reader ask their own question

    The ESG dashboards use parameters, filters and dashboard actions so someone can switch country, sector or year without asking me. A chart that only answers my question is a slide, not a tool.

  4. Check the record before it goes in

    At THIKNET a mis-placed road, turn restriction or EV charging point became a wrong instruction for every driver downstream. I learned to verify data against its source before committing it, and I still work that way in a spreadsheet or a model.
