Melbourne · R · 30,469 crashes

How much does the speed zone matter?

Read the case study

Pick a speed zone to see how many of every 100 pedestrian crashes killed or seriously injured someone.

Speed zone
Killed or seriously injuredOther injury
41 in 100

So what: moving a road from 60 to 40 km/h is linked to about 13 fewer serious injuries in every 100 pedestrian crashes, which is why the case study recommends 40 km/h zones in the inner-city hot spots.

Victorian police and hospital crash records, metro Melbourne 2012–2025. The rise across speed zones passes a chi-square test for trend (χ² = 274.9, p < 0.001). This is an association, not a controlled experiment: faster roads also differ in design and traffic.

Where would new pharmacies help most?

Read the case study

Add pharmacies chosen by location-allocation modelling and watch how many of the province's 511 villages fall within a 5 km service radius.

Pharmacies added
199 of 511 villages
0511 villages

So what: five well-placed pharmacies add 126 villages, so placement matters more than count. Today each participating pharmacist serves about 13,451 people, well above the NHSO guideline of 10,000, which is the case for adding them.

From the 2023 Asian Conference on Remote Sensing paper I co-authored. The paper also reports coverage as 82.74% (+5) and over 95% (+22); those percentages use a different coverage measure from the village counts shown here.
